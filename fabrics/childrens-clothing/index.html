Fabric

Children's clothing and pyjamas

Press the fabric that is actually in the garment, then respect the care label. Babywear and cool-iron polyester are not a normal cotton T-shirt.

These are starting points from the charts named below. Read the instructions for the product you are pressing, then test on a scrap or a spare blank. The number on a heat press dial is often not the true platen temperature.

Read the fibre and the care label first. A cotton school T-shirt can use the cotton HTV row when the label allows a hot press. A polyester football shirt or a printed pyjama top often will not.

Blank kids’ pyjamas in 100% cotton, about 200 gsm, have their own notes: HTV on the chest, embroidery, sublimation (it will not bond), Christmas sets and a size guide from 6–12 months to 5–6 years. At about 200 gsm, HI-5’s heavier row is the 150°C press in the table below, not the 120°C line.

Siser’s heat-sensitive article says HI-5 is laboratory tested and certified for baby clothing, and the product is OEKO-TEX Standard 100 Class I. That is a statement about the film, not a certificate for your finished pyjamas. EcoStretch is the low-temperature stretch film; it is listed for cotton, polyester and elastane, and the instruction sheet does not call it a babywear product.

Everyday EasyWeed at 150°C remains the cotton chart if the garment can take it. Say so on a test seam before you press a name across the chest. Keep plastic poppers, zips and existing prints off the platen. They melt or mark, and that is not a vinyl fault.

Sublimation only works on light polyester, at 190–205°C on the charts we cite. That is hot for a child’s top. Test a spare. DTF examples start around 140°C and go to 160°C. The cooler end is kinder, and it is still not a promise.

Common questions

Can I put glitter on pyjamas?

The glitter charts on this site start at 160°C. That is a poor first test on polyester nightwear. These sources do not publish a children’s glitter temperature. Test a seam only if you are willing to scrap the top.

Does pressing a design change nightwear safety labels?

No. If you sell children’s nightwear in the UK, the Nightwear (Safety) Regulations 1985 and later product-safety guidance still apply, including labelling such as “KEEP AWAY FROM FIRE” where it is required. Business Companion summarises the current trading-standards position. This site is not legal advice.
